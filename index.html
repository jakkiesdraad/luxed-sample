Composite, not a client Harbourline Systems does not exist. What the report contains

  • Cover and evidence base — sessions held, artefacts read, whose records the numbers came from.
  • Executive summary — the structural findings, what they cost, and the one thing to do first.
  • Operating model map — thirteen stages: where the work lives, who owns it, what breaks.
  • Readiness score — seven dimensions, the evidence behind each score, and one verdict sentence.
  • Bottleneck and opportunity matrix — every candidate workflow, sorted into initiatives and controls, with a costed table and a payback line.
  • 30-day roadmap — at most three initiatives and three controls, each with an owner, a first step and a number to watch. Plus one thing to stop doing.
  • What we didn't look at — the scope limits, in writing.
  • Next step — one option, one price.

What it costs to act on

The report prices every finding in the client's own numbers, and marks the rest not costed, because… — no market averages, no invented hours. In the sample, exactly one workflow had recorded hours:

Annual cost = hours a week × the client's loaded payroll rate × 46 working weeks. Payback against the R25,000 fee: 3.6 months.

The assessment itself is R25,000 — R15,000 for the first three businesses — and the fee is credited in full against implementation if you proceed within 60 days. Two working sessions, remote, plus a 30-minute walkthrough. Every other number in the report is either exposure (what one failure costs, kept out of the payback) or explicitly not costed.

What the verdict sentence looks like

The spine of the document Your operating model is ready for assisted capture and accelerated reporting in the project business. It will break at support, release and permission — the three places where nothing is written down and nobody owns the outcome.

Not a maturity band, not a heat map. A specific claim about what will work now and where the failure will come from, that a reader can argue with.

What it deliberately leaves out

  • Your numbers, your names, your contracts — the report is only worth anything with those in it, which is what the two sessions produce.
  • Outcomes. The sample claims no result for anyone, because it cannot: it is a composite.
  • Implementation pricing. It depends on what the diagnosis finds, so it is quoted after the document rather than before it.
  • Anything we could not source from your records. Where a rate or an hour was unavailable, the report says so instead of substituting a market average.
